# Real-World Extension: SelfRegulationSCP2

As a small real-world extension, I evaluated SelfRegulationSCP2, a long multivariate time-series classification dataset from the UEA archive. Unlike the synthetic tasks, the real-world classification task did not show catastrophic collapse under longer test lengths. Across positional encodings, mean accuracy remained around 49-54% from length 256 to the full length of 1152. This suggests that the severe failures observed in the synthetic benchmark are especially linked to exact algorithmic sequence transformations, while real-world classification may remain usable because partial or local features are still informative.![](selfregulationscp2_accuracy_mean_se.png)
*Plot 01: selfregulationscp2 accuracy vs length*

Main interpretation:
* Accuracy stays roughly between **49% and 56%** across lengths.
* There is **no catastrophic length collapse** like in synthetic sequence-to-sequence tasks.
* Learned and sinusoidal are slightly best at full length, both around **53.52%**.
* RoPE peaks at length 512, around **55.56%**, but drops at full length.
* NoPE has the largest full-length variation across seeds, with std around **6.86** at length 1152.
* ALiBi is stable but modest.


The SelfRegulationSCP2 plot shows classification accuracy across increasing test sequence lengths for five positional encodings. Unlike the synthetic tasks, accuracy does not collapse when the input becomes longer than the training length. Most models remain around 49-54% accuracy from length 256 to the full sequence length of 1152, with some seed variation. This suggests that real-world classification behaves differently from exact algorithmic sequence tasks: even if the model does not learn perfect length-general rules, it can still use informative local or partial patterns in longer inputs.
